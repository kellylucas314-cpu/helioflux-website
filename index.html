02Our approach

Cancer has a light signature.

Cells emit very faint light during metabolism. In laboratory and animal studies, cancer and comparison cells showed differences in their light emissions. HelioFlux is investigating whether those differences could support earlier cancer detection.

Dr. Nirosha J. Murugan spent a decade building the technology that reads these signals.

We built the tool to read it.

We've built research prototypes and published the early findings in peer-reviewed journals. Now we're developing the clinical version.

Hardware

QSense™

A photon-counting sensor designed to record the faint light living tissue gives off on its own, in darkness, with no dye and no added radiation.

Research prototype

Software

LuminAI™

Software built on a decade of cell and animal data, designed to read the color and timing of the signal, not only its brightness. Human cancer performance has not yet been established.

04The next study

Next: the first study in people with cancer.

Each person’s light reading would be compared with their biopsy result. That kind of human cancer data doesn’t exist yet. Producing it is the point of the study.

Where we’re starting

IRB study in development

Breast cancer detection

HelioFlux is preparing an IRB study for breast cancer detection with Dr. Tom Lomis, a world-renowned cancer oncologist and surgeon, to evaluate how ultra-weak photon emissions may support earlier, non-invasive detection.

Human study in preparation

Skin cancer detection

First human study targeting Q4 2026 with a Beverly Hills dermatology partner.

Published 2025 · iScience

Brain activity

In 20 healthy adults, faint light recorded near the head was studied alongside EEG. Published in iScience in 2025, it was an exploratory study of brain activity, not cancer detection.
